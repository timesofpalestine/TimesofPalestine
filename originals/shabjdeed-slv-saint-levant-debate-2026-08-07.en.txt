One song has set off the loudest conversation in Arab music this year. On 1 August the Ramallah rapper Shabjdeed released "SLV" on his BLTNM label, a drill track produced by Smokaholic that takes direct aim at Saint Levant — the Gaza-raised star whose forty-city world tour opens next month.

!video[Shabjdeed — SLV (prod. Smokaholic), the track that opened the debate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zaswtLBWUX4)

The track questions how Palestinian identity travels to the world's stages, Cairo's Ahram Online reported: less an attack on Saint Levant's Palestinianness than on what the song hears as a gap between a polished political image and a catalogue of romance built for a global audience. Middle East Eye distilled the argument to a single question — who gets to represent Palestine?

> Who gets to represent Palestine?
> The question Middle East Eye drew from "SLV"

Rolling Stone MENA read the exchange differently, as a rejection of the "perfect victim" — a scene refusing to let one image of Palestinians, suffering nobly for outside sympathy, stand in for the artists still living and working under occupation.

Saint Levant had not answered publicly by Thursday, and his record supplies the reply his fans cite: an album named DEIRA for the hotel his father built on Gaza City's seafront — from mud, because cement could not enter — and a tour that carries Gaza's name across four continents.

The timing sharpens everything. Both artists play Amman on 13 August, in separate shows the same night, The New Arab noted — two rooms in one city, holding two answers to the same question.
