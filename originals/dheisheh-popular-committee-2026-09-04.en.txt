Naji Owdah runs the Phoenix Center in Dheisheh refugee camp near Bethlehem and coordinates the camp's Popular Committee, one of nineteen such volunteer bodies UNRWA and the Palestinian Authority do not directly control across the West Bank's refugee camps, according to IMEMC News.

Dheisheh holds roughly 13,000 people on under half a square kilometre. Established in 1995 under PLO oversight, its Popular Committee runs on volunteers who meet monthly with counterparts from the other camps and coordinate year-round.

## Where the committee steps in

The committee's mandate covers water and infrastructure repairs, help arranging surgeries and medical care, scholarships and other education aid, short-term job programmes and food assistance for residents UNRWA's own services do not reach, IMEMC reported.

When a contaminated water supply once made residents sick, UNRWA and municipal authorities did not move fast enough on their own. The committee ran what Owdah described as a pressure campaign: interviews, newspaper columns and photographs of the problem, until the repair was funded.

The same model runs in other camps under different names. Ahmed Dhuqan heads the Popular Committee for Services in Nablus's Balata camp, home to an Islamic alms committee linked to Nablus's own zakat body, the Jaffa Cultural Center founded in 1996, a Women's Activity Center, and the Yazour Charitable Society, among other resident-run bodies.

## What UNRWA itself is short of

The committees exist because UNRWA's own resources are stretched thin. The agency's 2026 occupied Palestinian territory Flash Appeal seeks 1.26 billion dollars to keep serving an estimated 2.4 million people across Gaza and the West Bank.

The strain shows most starkly north of Dheisheh. UNRWA's own satellite assessment found roughly 1,460 structures destroyed or damaged across Jenin, Nur Shams and Tulkarm camps — 52 percent of Jenin's structures, 48 percent of Nur Shams's 382 damaged buildings and 36 percent of Tulkarm's 402.

The Israeli forces operation that began in the northern West Bank in January 2025 displaced some 40,000 people, the largest such displacement in the West Bank since 1967, UNRWA said.

Dheisheh has not been struck at that scale. But the same funding pressure that left Jenin's camps waiting on repairs is the reason Owdah's committee exists at all.

Volunteers do, block by block, the work a chronically underfunded UN agency cannot always reach in time. The committee also coordinates with SOS Bethlehem and the Arab Committee for Rehabilitation when a case needs specialist help beyond what its own volunteers can give.

## What still depends on the funding

None of what the committees do replaces UNRWA's mandate. Owdah's own role runs alongside it, not instead of it, and the agency remains the camp's main provider of schooling and primary care.

What the committees add is speed and a resident's eye for what is broken first. Whether that model can keep pace now depends on a UNRWA appeal that, like the rest of its 2026 budget, is not yet fully funded.
