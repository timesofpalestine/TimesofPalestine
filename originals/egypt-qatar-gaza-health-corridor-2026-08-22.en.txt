Egypt's minister of health and population, Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, met Qatar's minister of state for international cooperation, Mariam bint Ali bin Nasser Al Misnad, in Cairo on 20 August to discuss widening the two countries' joint work on health, with the treatment of Palestinians from Gaza at the centre of it, according to a readout published by Egypt's State Information Service.

The Egyptian side put numbers to what has already been delivered. About 172 Egyptian hospitals have admitted Palestinian patients and wounded from Gaza, the health ministry said, and close to 3,000 surgical operations have been performed on them, alongside medical and psychological rehabilitation.

Those figures describe a corridor that has been running, quietly and at scale, since the first year of the war. They also describe its limit: a hospital bed in Cairo helps a patient who has reached Cairo.

## What Qatar has built inside Gaza, and what it cannot get in

Qatar's contribution has been physical as well as financial. Sheikh Hamad Hospital for Rehabilitation and Prosthetics in northern Gaza, opened in 2019 with money from the Qatar Fund for Development, is the Strip's main rehabilitation facility, with a hundred beds.

In April its general director, Ahmed Naim, said the hospital had received no prosthetics supplies since the war began and could offer maintenance on existing limbs only, because there are no local alternatives for the materials.

That is the shape of the file the two ministers were discussing. Money and expertise on the Arab side are available; what is scarce is passage — for the crates of plaster and plastic that a workshop in Gaza City needs, and for the patients whose names sit on evacuation lists. Reporting this month put the number waiting for medical evacuation at between fifteen and eighteen thousand.

## The next practical stage

Neither ministry announced a new fund, a signed agreement or a timetable, and the readout should be read as what it was: a meeting about deepening work already under way.

The measure that will matter is arithmetic, not language. If the Cairo corridor's next quarter shows more patients crossing, and if a shipment of prosthetics materials reaches Sheikh Hamad Hospital, the meeting will have been the start of something. Those are countable things, and this paper will count them.
