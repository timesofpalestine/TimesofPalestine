Ashraf Dabbour, the former Palestinian Authority ambassador arrested at Beirut airport on Monday, is being held at an Information Branch facility of Lebanon's Internal Security Forces in the city's Achrafieh district, and his health has deteriorated in custody — he has since been moved to a hospital, people following his case told Times of Palestine on Tuesday evening.

The same sources gave this newspaper a graver account of the arrest itself. Yasser Abbas, the president's son, coordinated the detention with contacts inside the Lebanese state, they said, and the aim is to transfer the former envoy to Ramallah to face charges the sources describe as fabricated — punishment for the accusation Dabbour made against the son in April.

Times of Palestine has not been able to verify that account, and sets out plainly what stands against it. The official record says a Lebanese judicial official confirmed to AFP that Dabbour was held on a Palestinian warrant, ahead of extradition on charges of embezzling funds during his tenure. No court has ever convicted Yasser Abbas of any crime, and he has maintained through two decades of scrutiny that his affairs are lawful.

## The favour the sources describe

The sources tie the alleged cooperation to a story that, they say, has circulated in Palestinian diplomatic circles for years: that Yasser Abbas provided Palestinian diplomatic passports to Lebanese officials, who used them to enter African countries — and, in earlier years, Venezuela — as Palestinians to set up businesses there. In this telling, the arrest is partly repayment for those favours.

That is the weakest tier of tonight's record, and this newspaper marks it so. No document supports the passport account, no named official confirms it, and it reaches us second-hand. It runs here as an unverified allegation, labelled as such, because it is now part of how the case is being read in Beirut and Ramallah alike — and because no institution exists on the Palestinian side to establish or dispel it.

## What the record already establishes

Dabbour served as ambassador in Beirut from 2012 until 2025, when President Mahmoud Abbas dismissed him after handing his son the mandate over the sale of the PLO's land and properties in Lebanon, a transfer of authority reported at the time by Asharq Al-Awsat. In April the dismissed envoy named his adversary on his own Facebook page.

> He is exploiting his control over the security services and his influence within the Authority to settle his personal scores — pursuing me with arrest warrants from one place to another.
> Ashraf Dabbour on Yasser Abbas, Facebook, April 2026

On Monday, Lebanese judges acted on the Ramallah warrant when his plane landed, as [this newspaper reported](https://www.timesofpalestine.com/en/story/eb10f3b698.html). Palestinian prosecutors charge him with corruption and embezzlement during his tenure; Dabbour denies the charges and says they are designed to tarnish his reputation. No trial has yet tested either account.

The man the sources now place behind the arrest carries [a two-decade documented file](https://www.timesofpalestine.com/en/story/4e50a087e8.html): the Falcon Holding conglomerate built inside his father's system, the USAID contracts entered into the US congressional record, and the libel suit he brought against Foreign Policy over the questions it asked — and lost, in courts that ruled the asking lawful without ruling on the answers.

## The forum that exists, and the one that does not

For now the only working forum is Lebanese. Extradition requires a Lebanese judicial decision, Dabbour's lawyers can contest the warrant before it, and his condition in custody — a man moved from an Achrafieh detention facility to a hospital within a day of his arrest — is a matter Lebanese judges can be asked to weigh.

On the Palestinian side, no equivalent exists. There has been no sitting parliament since 2007, the security services answer to presidential decree alone, and no oversight body has answered Dabbour's April accusation or examined the mandates the president has placed in his son's hands. If tonight's account from the sources is wrong, only an institution could establish that; if it is right, only an institution could act on it. That absence — which no rumour created and no denial cures — remains the one certainty in this file.
