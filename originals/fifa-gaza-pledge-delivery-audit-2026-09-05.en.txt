title: FIFA's $75 million Gaza football pledge is mostly still on paper

FIFA and Donald Trump announced a $75 million fund for Palestinian football in February 2026, a package the Palestinian Football Association counts against 265 sporting facilities damaged or destroyed and more than a thousand members of its sporting community killed since the war began.

FIFA president Gianni Infantino set out the fund's shape at the announcement: $50 million for a stadium holding 20,000 to 25,000 people, $15 million for a FIFA academy, $2.5 million for fifty "mini pitches," and $1 million each for five full-sized fields. The plan rides alongside the Trump-backed Board of Peace's broader Gaza reconstruction framework.

## What separates a pledge from a pitch

Seven months on, FIFA has not published a disbursement ledger showing what portion of the $75 million has actually reached a contractor, a relief agency or the Palestinian Football Association itself. No completed stadium, academy building or full-sized field has been documented as finished; FIFA's own public statements since February describe the fund's scope, not its spending to date.

Coverage at the announcement noted the pledge caught FIFA's own membership by surprise, and commentary since has questioned whether a stadium is the association's most urgent need against a casualty and destruction count that includes youth academies, training grounds and neighbourhood pitches used well beyond competitive football.

## What the PFA says it needs now

The Palestinian Football Association's damage count — 265 facilities, more than a thousand people from the sporting community killed — sets the scale FIFA's fund is meant to answer. Rebuilding neighbourhood pitches and restoring salaries for displaced federation staff and coaches are the association's stated near-term priorities, ahead of a single marquee stadium, though FIFA has not detailed a phased spending plan that sequences the two.

## The next practical stage

An itemised disbursement account — what has been paid, to which contractor or programme, and what physical work it funded — would let the PFA's damage count and FIFA's pledge be measured against each other. Until FIFA publishes one, the $75 million remains a commitment whose delivery cannot yet be checked against Gaza's football grounds.
