CBS News' foreign editor, Shayndi Raice, told the network's foreign staff that allegations against Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu were "a sideshow" that "nobody really cares" about, and called the Palestinian death toll in Gaza a "magic number," according to leaked audio of the meeting obtained by the media newsletter Status.

The virtual meeting, held on Tuesday, 13 October, and recorded without the participants' knowledge, turned on a CBS Evening News report by senior foreign correspondent Holly Williams marking the third anniversary of the Hamas-led attack of 7 October 2023. Status reporter Oliver Darcy published the account, and TheWrap and Mediaite carried its quotes.

## What was cut, and why

Williams told the meeting that she had been ordered to make changes to her script as written, with no room to negotiate. Her biggest objection, she said, was not the death toll but the removal of allegations involving Netanyahu.

Raice defended the cut on three grounds, according to the recording: CBS News had not independently verified the allegations, Netanyahu denied them, and the extra context made the segment too complicated.

> "Those allegations are a sideshow. It's not important, and I just think you got it wrong."
> Shayndi Raice to Holly Williams, in leaked audio published by Status

She went further. "I was just in Israel for Sukkot," Raice said. "Because nobody really cares. That is not an issue in the election." She told Williams she had probably got the story wrong "because you don't know the story as well as I do."

## The "magic number"

On Gaza, Raice called the toll of more than 74,000 Palestinians killed, the count kept by the Gaza Health Ministry, a "magic number." She described repeatedly citing it in stories as "posturing" and argued that including it was "not saving Palestinians," Status reported.

Raice denied ordering the figure removed from Williams's report. She said she had instead asked that the Israeli death toll from 7 October also be included, and that Palestinian casualties be split between civilians and combatants.

The Gaza Health Ministry's count does not distinguish civilians from fighters. United Nations agencies have relied on its figures throughout the war, and Israel disputes them.

## "Legal grounds for firing"

The meeting ended with a warning to the whole foreign desk. "If you can't answer to that hierarchy, and you don't listen to what we say, that is called insubordination, and it's legal grounds for firing," Raice said, according to the audio.

One attendee told Darcy the remarks left "jaws on the floor." Status reported that Williams is weighing whether to stay at the network. CBS News did not respond to requests for comment from Status or TheWrap.

## Bari Weiss's foreign desk

Raice joined CBS News in May from The Wall Street Journal, where she was deputy bureau chief for the Middle East and North Africa. Bari Weiss, the network's editor-in-chief, and CBS News president Tom Cibrowski announced the hire, and she reports to Wendy Fisher, senior vice president of editorial for CBS News and Stations, TheWrap reported.

Her appointment followed the departure of London bureau chief Claire Day, a 25-year CBS veteran. The New York Post, citing two sources, reported that Day left after clashing with Weiss over coverage of the wars in Gaza and Iran. Status reported that Weiss saw the London bureau as so biased against Israel that it "might as well be Hamas." CBS did not publicly give a reason for Day's exit.

## The October 7 record in Israel

The dispute came in the same week that Israel's own press was dominated by 7 October allegations against Netanyahu's office. Avi Gil, Netanyahu's former military secretary, said in his first interview since the attack that the prime minister's office had sought to alter the recorded time of an early-morning call on 7 October, moving it from 6:40 to 6:29.

Israeli police are investigating whether senior officials tried to change cabinet protocols and call transcripts from the war's first days, and Netanyahu's former chief of staff, Tzachi Braverman, is among the suspects, according to Ynet. Netanyahu and Likud reject the accusation as a politically motivated campaign.
