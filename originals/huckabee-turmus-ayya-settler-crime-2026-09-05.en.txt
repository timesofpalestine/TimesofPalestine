US ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee visited the Palestinian town of Turmus Ayya on Friday, days after extremist settlers sprayed swastika graffiti targeting him, and told residents there that "a crime is a crime, terror is terror," the Israeli news site Ynet reported.

Huckabee, a longtime supporter of the settlement enterprise, met families in the town near Ramallah, where Ynet noted roughly 80 percent of residents hold US citizenship. The visit followed a spike in settler violence incidents that Ynet's report tied directly to the graffiti attack targeting the ambassador himself.

## What the visit signals

The Israeli daily Maariv, in its own report on the visit, framed it against "a proliferation of settler-violence incidents," noting Huckabee had recently launched a sharp public attack on what he called "Israeli terrorists" — unusually blunt language from a US ambassador known for backing the settlement movement.

Neither outlet reported that Huckabee announced any policy step beyond the visit itself and his on-the-record remarks. The State Department has separately called for "severe consequences" to stop West Bank settler violence after meeting Palestinian-Americans who reported attacks, a position Huckabee's visit echoes without adding new detail on what those consequences would be.

## Why an Israeli outlet carried it this way

Ynet's own framing noted the graffiti attack came from "extremist settlers," language distinguishing the vandals from the settlement movement broadly — a distinction the ambassador's visit implicitly reinforced by directing his warning at people who "commit crimes" rather than settlers as a category.
