Three Arab states put something physical into Gaza this week, and each delivery can be counted.

A Saudi convoy carrying a large consignment of tents reached the Strip on Tuesday, sent by the King Salman Humanitarian Aid and Relief Centre under the Saudi popular campaign for the Palestinian people, the Saudi Press Agency reported.

The centre named its implementing partner inside Gaza: the Saudi Centre for Culture and Heritage, which takes delivery and directs the supplies according to need priority. That detail matters more than it looks. A pledge with no named receiving body in Gaza is a press release; a pledge with one is a distribution chain.

## Egypt's convoy numbers keep climbing

Egypt's "Zad al-Izza" convoys are now past their two hundred and fiftieth run. Egyptian state media reported the 257th convoy entering on 16 August and the 258th loading behind it, carrying food, medical requirements, therapeutic medicines and the fuel that keeps Gaza's remaining functioning facilities running.

The route is the one the war left: south through the Rafah land port, then east to Kerem Shalom for Israeli inspection before entry. Egypt has continued receiving wounded Palestinians in the other direction through the same crossing.

Jordan's field hospital in southern Gaza — the third the kingdom has run in the Strip — distributed food to residents this week alongside its clinical work, Jordanian state television reported.

## What is delivered and what is still promised

The charter of this section is to separate those two things, and the gap remains the story.

Tents, trucks and operating theatres are delivered. The Gaza reconstruction money is not: as Times of Palestine reported on 13 August, Arab states have pledged $4.2 billion to the World Bank-administered Gaza fund and the fund has not yet received it.

The direction of the aid flow is also worth stating honestly. Compiled United Nations figures cited by the Council on Foreign Relations show total aid entering Gaza fell by 37 percent in the first three months of 2026 against the level reached after the October 2025 ceasefire — a decline into which this week's convoys arrive.

That is the shape of Arab support at the moment: humanitarian delivery that is real, continuous and countable, running alongside reconstruction financing that has been announced but not moved. The first keeps people alive through another summer. Only the second rebuilds anything.
