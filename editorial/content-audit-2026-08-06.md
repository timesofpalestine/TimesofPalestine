# Content audit & story recommendations — 2026-08-06

A full scan of the site's content inventory against the August 2026 news
landscape and the audiences Times of Palestine serves. Prepared by Claude at
the owner's request. Recommendations only — nothing here changes coverage by
itself; the daily editor cycle and the other desks should draw assignments
from it, and disagreements go to issue #6.

## 1. What the site is today (inventory)

  16 reader-facing sections, 67 wire feeds (46 EN / 21 AR), plus the
  franchises: Washington Brief, TOP 100 (2026 edition live), the Palestinian

## 2. The market this site publishes into (researched 2026-08-06)

- **Ceasefire, not peace.** The Gaza ceasefire is near day 300 and "far from
  perfect" (UN Security Council briefings, May 2026): roughly 0.5% of rubble
  cleared, most donor pledges **not yet transferred** to the World
  Bank-administered reconstruction fund, ~80% of buildings damaged or
  destroyed, over a million people without permanent shelter. The Board of
  Peace (chaired by President Trump, with Nikolay Mladenov as
  Director-General/High Representative) and the Palestinian technocratic
  committee (NCAG) were named in January 2026 but are **not operational on
  the ground** — a governance vacuum that is itself the story.
- **Two elections define the next four months.** Israel votes 27 October
  (opposition bloc polling ~58–60 seats vs ~50–52 for Netanyahu's coalition;
  Eisenkot's Yashar running neck-and-neck with Likud; the Arab lists the
  kingmaker question). Palestinian Legislative Council elections are set for
  **28 November 2026**, with PNC elections the same month and a presidential
  vote promised for early 2027 — the first Palestinian national elections in
  two decades, run under a contested framework that excludes Hamas and
  Islamic Jihad and under a new draft constitution.
- **The West Bank is the escalation story.** OCHA counts 1,330+
  settler-involved attacks since January; the UN says settler violence is at
  an "all-time high"; ~1,988 Palestinians displaced in the first four months
  of 2026 (already 20% above all of 2025); the security cabinet approved
  ₪1.3bn for 34 new settlements; Amnesty published an "ethnic cleansing"
  finding in June. Our westbank section holds **two** originals.
- **A third lost school year begins September 1.** 658,000 Gaza children,
  60% with no in-person learning, 92% of schools needing rebuild or major
  rehabilitation, 335,000 under-fives at risk of developmental delay
  (UNICEF/PCHR). Education is simultaneously a Gaza story, a health story
  and a diaspora-donor story.
- **The audience is diaspora-heavy, social-first and distrustful of legacy
  media.** Research through 2026 shows the Palestinian diaspora shifting to
  citizen journalism and social platforms out of perceived mainstream bias,
  Gaza creators publishing in English to reach global audiences, and
  Arabic-language news systematically underserved by AI-driven search and
  answer engines. Service journalism, data people can cite, and native
  Arabic quality are the differentiators available to us.

## 3. Priority recommendations

### A. Stand up a Palestinian elections desk — now, not in November

The 28 November PLC election plus the PNC vote plus the early-2027
presidential promise is the biggest domestic Palestinian story since 2006,
and it starts as a **process** story months before it is a results story.
No queue topic currently covers the announced elections as scheduled fact
(`palestinian-elections` and `election-machinery-2026` predate the date
announcement — sharpen both). Standing coverage should track: voter
registration mechanics in the West Bank, Gaza and East Jerusalem (the
East Jerusalem question is where past elections died); the legal basis and
the Hamas-exclusion controversy with all sides carried; the new draft
constitution, explained clause by clause; candidate lists as they form; how
9+ million diaspora Palestinians are (or are not) represented in the PNC
vote; and whether an election under occupation and without Gaza's full
participation can confer the legitimacy the statehood-recognition wave
demands. Mirror of the Israeli election watch, same tracker treatment,
launch report + weekly sweep. **Suggested launch:**
`originals/palestinian-elections-2026-desk.*`.

### B. Where is the money? — a reconstruction-funds tracker

The single most consequential accountability story in the market: pledges
announced vs money actually transferred to the World Bank fund vs rubble
actually cleared vs shelters actually built. Every number attributed to the
specific UNSC briefing, World Bank report or donor statement, refreshed on a
cadence, with a house infographic that other people cite. This fuses three
existing strengths (accountability, arabaid pledge-tracking, the
`gaza-reconstruction-money` queue topic) into a recurring franchise rather
than a one-off. Companion beat: a **Board of Peace / NCAG watch** — who sits
on these bodies, what they have decided, what authority Palestinians
actually hold inside them, and the gap between the January announcements and
the ground. **Suggested launch:** `originals/gaza-reconstruction-money-tracker-2026.*`
plus a standing SVG dashboard updated with each edition.

### C. A West Bank displacement ledger

Two originals in the section during the worst settler-violence year on
record is the site's largest single mismatch between news weight and
coverage weight. Recommend a monthly data-journalism fixture: OCHA's attack
and displacement counts, a village-by-village record of communities emptied
(names, dates, what stood there), the settlement-budget money trail, and the
impunity record (complaints filed vs indictments), each attributed to OCHA /
UN OHCHR / Yesh Din / B'Tselem by report and date. The queue's
`settler-violence-impunity`, `area-c-demolitions`, `west-bank-camp-
displacement-machinery` topics all feed this; the ledger gives them a
permanent, citable home. Active-voice titles will name the actors — this
beat is where the house no-passive rule earns its keep.

### D. September 1: the third lost school year (planned package, not a reaction)

A flagship bilingual package timed to the school-year start: the numbers
(UNICEF, PCHR, ETF — attributed and dated), what "Back to Learning" actually
reaches (336,000 of 658,000), the university students stranded mid-degree,
the under-five developmental emergency, and the solutions lens the health
series proved out — tent classrooms, remote degrees, the scholarship guide
cross-linked as the service layer. Then keep a term-time cadence, not a
single splash. **Suggested slugs:** `originals/gaza-third-lost-school-year-2026.*`,
`originals/gaza-university-students-stranded-2026.*`. This also completes the
health series' natural arc (`gaza-polio-vaccination-rebuild` and
`gaza-rehabilitation-capacity` are still queued — finish them first).

### F. Feed the starving sections

- **Sport (0 originals).** Football is the diaspora's shared ritual and the
  cheapest engagement win on the board. Immediate pieces: where Palestinian
  football stands after the 2026 World Cup qualification cycle (the
  federation's historic run — verify final results at writing); the
  West Bank Premier League's attempt to play a season under closure; Gaza's
  athletes one year into the ceasefire; Palestinian athletes' travel-permit
  battles (queued as `athletes-and-travel` — promote it). Aim: 4+ originals
  by end of September so the section stops depending on wire luck.
- **From the Archive (0 items).** The owner holds full rights to the
  2006–07 Palestine Times — a differentiator literally no competitor can
  copy — and not one piece is republished. Recommend a weekly fixture
  (suggest Sundays, complementing the Friday Table): one archive piece,
  original date and byline honored per charter, paired with a short
  present-day note elsewhere on the page about what changed in twenty years.
  Needs owner-supplied scans only; no agent scraping.
- **Real Lives / Field Reports (2 + 1 originals).** The ceasefire's
  "neither war nor peace" texture is this section: the family rebuilding a
  bakery over cleared rubble, the tent-city economies, returns to Rafah,
  the fisherman testing the new zone limit. One humans original per week is
  a realistic floor; We Are Not Numbers already feeds the wire side.
- **Her Story (4).** Keep the pipeline warm with the UN documentation
  cycle: the queued `women-and-violence` topic, women-headed households in
  the reconstruction economy, women running the tent schools of package D.

### G. Grow the Arabic edition's own legs

Charter already mandates native-Arabic quality; the structural gap is
inputs: 21 AR feeds vs 46 EN, and no Arabic equivalents of the dedicated
health/humans/women/opinion feeds. Recommend: add Arabic feeds for those
beats (candidates: وفا's health desk, أصوات نسوية sources, the Arabic
rights-org wires), and commission 2–3 Arabic-first originals per month —
stories reported for the Arabic reader and then rendered into English,
reversing the usual direction (the Arab Support and Her Story beats are the
natural home). This is also the answer to the documented Arabic data gap in
AI search: clean, well-structured native Arabic journalism is scarce supply.

### H. Service journalism as an SEO/AI-era moat

The scholarship guide and embassies directory prove the model: evergreen
reference pages that answer questions people actually search, kept current
by the daily cycle. Extend the family, one per fortnight: **sending money
to Gaza and the West Bank** (remittance channels that work, fees, the
financial-freedom desk's beat grafted in — coordinate with ChatGPT's
layer); **Palestinian documents explained** (IDs, family reunification,
East Jerusalem residency — pairs with the queued `jerusalem-residency`);
**a verified donation guide** (which Gaza appeals are audited — pure
reader-protection accountability); **medical evacuation pathways** (who
qualifies, which corridors function — grafts onto the health series).
Deadlines and eligibility always verify-at-source, per charter.

### I′. Freshness is a hard product guarantee (owner order 2026-08-06, added same day)

The owner observed the front page's latest item sitting at **seven hours
old** — a direct violation of the charter's guiding principle. Diagnosis
(Actions history, 2026-08-06): a GitHub-side outage from ~15:30 UTC
("Service Unavailable" resolving actions) failed three builds in a row,
then killed the 16:46 `rearm.yml` run — whose single no-retry `curl` was
the whole cadence chain. With rearm dead and the cron schedule throttled
to one tick every 3–4 hours, **no build ran from 16:18 to ~20:15**, and
the site froze at the 15:14 deploy.

Standing rule going forward: **"Latest" is minutes old, never hours.**
The machinery that guarantees it (implemented with this audit):

- `rearm.yml` hardened — retries on every API call, and fail-open: if the
  idle-check can't be read, dispatch anyway (a duplicate dispatch queues
  harmlessly behind the `pages-build` concurrency group; a missed one
  freezes the site). Dispatch itself retries 6× over ~5 minutes.
- New `heartbeat.yml` — an independent watchdog on its own cron stream
  that also fires after every scheduled desk workflow (daily editor,
  Washington Brief, Bitcoin/Diaspora dispatches). If no build is queued,
  running, or started in the last 20 minutes, it dispatches one. A dead
  chain now revives at the next heartbeat tick instead of staying dark.
- Both respect the existing `SELF_REARM=off` pause switch and the same
  `WORKFLOW_DISPATCH_TOKEN` secret; without the token they exit quietly
  and say so in the log.

Residual risk, stated honestly: during a **full** GitHub Actions outage
nothing inside GitHub can run, and staleness lasts until GitHub recovers —
the heartbeat then catches it within minutes of recovery. If the owner
wants a watchdog outside GitHub entirely (an external scheduler pinging
the dispatch API, or a scheduled Claude Routine checking site freshness
hourly), that is a small add but carries its own recurring cost —
owner's call; say the word and it ships.

## 5. What NOT to add

- No new sections. Sixteen is enough; the gaps above are filling existing
  sections, not creating surfaces. A Palestinian-elections *desk* lives in
  politics; the reconstruction tracker lives in accountability.
- No commentary/hot-take expansion. The market is saturated with opinion;
  the site's edge is verified, attributed, bilingual reporting and
  reference material. Opinion stays wire-fed.
- No pace increase on the investigations desk (3/day is owner-set); the
  recommendations above mostly re-aim existing capacity via topics.json and
  the daily editor cycle rather than adding spend.
