# Palestinian Public Ledger

Owner directive: 2026-07-30

Cadence: one report every 14 days, plus one audited annual ranking after the
latest comparable company filings and public accounts are available.

This is a forensic public-finance and economic-power desk. It is independent
journalism, not a state anti-corruption office, criminal investigation or
substitute for an audit. Its purpose is to make public records comparable,
identify discrepancies that deserve answers, and show readers where public
money and economic power sit.

## Core questions

Every report should answer as many of these questions as the records permit:

1. What did the government budget?
2. What revenue and expenditure did it recognize in its accounts?
3. What cash actually moved?
4. What became debt, arrears, receivables, withheld revenue or net lending?
5. Which public body, bank, supplier, contractor, state-owned enterprise or
   listed company ultimately received or controlled the money?
6. What changed from the closest genuinely comparable earlier period?
7. What cannot be known from the disclosures, and which record would answer
   it?

## Source hierarchy

Start with primary records:

- Ministry of Finance budget laws, monthly and annual fiscal tables, final
  accounts, debt bulletins and procurement disclosures
- State Audit and Administrative Control Bureau reports and follow-up reports
- Palestinian Monetary Authority banking, credit and financial-stability data
- Palestine Exchange and Palestinian Capital Market Authority filings
- audited company reports, shareholder registers and dividend notices
- Palestinian Investment Fund, pension authority, municipalities, utilities
  and state-owned-enterprise accounts
- official donor, World Bank, IMF, EU, UN and OECD records
- court filings, company registries, procurement portals and competition
  decisions where publicly available

Use reporting and expert analysis to find questions and test interpretations,
not to replace the underlying record. Preserve the title, publisher, date,
language, URL, access date, table or page number, unit, accounting basis and
the claim each source supports in the internal research ledger.

## Comparison rules

Do not put two numbers in the same chart until these fields are reconciled:

- period covered and whether it is complete
- central government, general government or another institutional scope
- cash, commitment, accrual or modified-cash accounting basis
- preliminary, audited or final status
- gross or net presentation
- nominal currency and exchange rate
- population and GDP denominator
- inflation and any rebasing
- classification changes, including transfers, fuel subsidies and net lending
- treatment of Gaza, withheld clearance revenue, refunds, deductions and
  arrears

If a valid conversion is not possible, show the figures in separate columns
and explain why. Never imply that a difference caused by accounting scope is
missing money.

## Discrepancy standard

Use precise labels:

- **Reconciled difference:** the records explain it.
- **Timing difference:** recognition and payment occur in different periods.
- **Classification difference:** the same transaction is recorded differently.
- **Disclosure gap:** a required or expected record is not public or is late.
- **Unresolved discrepancy:** comparable records conflict after reasonable
  reconciliation.
- **Allegation:** a named source or authority makes a claim that Times of
  Palestine has not independently proved.

No discrepancy becomes an accusation through rhetoric. Before suggesting
misconduct, obtain corroborating evidence, test innocent explanations, seek a
meaningful response through a public contact channel, and allow reasonable
time. State the question asked, the date and method, and the response or lack
of one in the internal ledger. If the evidence or response period is
insufficient, hold the allegation and publish the verified system-level facts.

## Annual Palestinian Profit and Power Ranking

The ranking covers companies and publicly documented institutions, not a
speculative list of wealthy people.

Publish separate, clearly labelled tables for:

1. audited net profit attributable to shareholders
2. revenue
3. dividends paid or declared
4. return on equity
5. market capitalization at a stated date
6. public-contract receipts where procurement records disclose them
7. banking exposure to government and major economic sectors
8. ownership or control supported by public filings

For every entry show the fiscal year, currency, audit status, consolidation
scope and filing link. Do not mix banks, insurers and operating companies in a
single profitability ratio without explaining the sector difference. Do not
double-count subsidiaries. A table of entities that could not be ranked
because filings were missing, late or incomparable is part of the result.

Name a controlling shareholder or beneficial owner only when a current public
filing proves the relationship. Distinguish ownership, board membership,
management, related-party transactions and political office. None alone proves
improper influence.

## Publication shape

Each bilingual pair should include:

- one short active headline in each language
- a clear finding in the opening, with the accounting basis and period
- a compact comparison table or chart
- a story-specific lead visual and, for data-heavy work, one explanatory
  in-body visual
- inline links to the records that support material claims
- a concise methodology paragraph in the article, not a bibliography
- a fair account of explanations and responses
- native Arabic composition and a separate Arabic-only edit

Keep the detailed source ledger and calculations internal. Published articles
follow the repository contract: attribution and links appear in the prose;
there is no sources or bibliography section.

## Initial sequence

1. **Two ledgers:** 2010 under Salam Fayyad versus 2025, separating recognized
   revenue and expenditure from cash, receivables, arrears and debt.
2. **Audit follow-through:** which State Audit Bureau recommendations were
   implemented, repeated or left unresolved.
3. **Procurement recipients:** what public contract records disclose about
   repeat suppliers and concentration.
4. **Public-sector finance:** bank exposure to the PA, arrears to suppliers
   and the pension system, and who bears the financing gap.
5. **Public assets and monopolies:** PIF, telecoms, fuel, cement, utilities and
   the limits of available ownership data.
6. **Profit and power:** the annual audited company ranking and an explicit
   list of entities that disclosure gaps make unrankable.

The sequence changes when a correction, audit finding, new final account or
public-interest disclosure is more urgent. Check `topics.json`, recent
`originals/`, the live site and issue #6 before selecting an angle.
